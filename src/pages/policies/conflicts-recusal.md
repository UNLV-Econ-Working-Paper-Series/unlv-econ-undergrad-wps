## General standard

Editors and editorial team members must disclose circumstances that could reasonably affect—or appear to affect—their impartiality. The remaining non-conflicted voting editors determine whether recusal is required.

A conflict may arise when an editor:

- taught the author or authors;
- currently supervises or directly mentors an author;
- serves as the paper's faculty sponsor;
- has coauthored with an author or contributed substantially to the paper;
- has a close personal or family relationship with an author;
- has an employment or financial relationship connected to an author, organization, dataset, or outcome;
- is involved in an active dispute with an author;
- has previously advocated for or against the paper in a way that prevents impartial review; or
- otherwise believes they cannot remain impartial.

Teaching, supervision, or faculty sponsorship does not automatically prevent all participation in mentorship. It does mean the relationship must be disclosed and the faculty sponsor cannot be the sole approving editor for a paper they directly supervised.

## Effect of recusal

A recused editor is removed from:

- deliberation about the paper or action;
- voting;
- appeal review; and
- access to confidential material that is not necessary for any remaining operational duty.

The private decision record identifies the disclosure, the recusal determination, the eligible voting pool, and any access restriction. The public paper page does not expose confidential personal details.

## Required independent approval

A paper must be approved by at least two non-conflicted voting editors, including at least one Faculty Editor. If fewer than two non-conflicted voting editors are available, the Editorial Board may appoint a qualified UNLV faculty member as an ad hoc reviewer for that paper.

Appeals are decided by a non-conflicted Faculty Editor who was not responsible for the original decision, or by an appointed ad hoc UNLV faculty reviewer.

## Questions or disclosures

Authors, faculty sponsors, editors, and readers may contact the Series through the [contact page](../../contact/) to disclose a potential conflict or request review. Include the paper title or URL, but do not send restricted research data or sensitive personal records through an ordinary email message.
